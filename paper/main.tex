\title[The Bohr radius of the bidisc]{A lower bound for the Bohr radius of the bidisc}
\author{Tejasvi Singh Tomar}
\renewcommand{\urladdrname}{{\itshape ORCID}}
\urladdr{\href{https://orcid.org/0000-0003-4668-3639}{https://orcid.org/0000-0003-4668-3639}}
\subjclass[2020]{32A05; 30B10, 42A05, 47A48}
\keywords{Bohr radius, bidisc, Carath\'eodory class, Sidon constant, transfer-function realization}

\begin{abstract}
The Bohr radius $K_2$ of the bidisc is the largest $r$ such that every holomorphic $f(z)=\sum c_\alpha z^\alpha$ on $\D^2$ with $|f|\le1$ satisfies $\sum|c_\alpha|r^{|\alpha|}\le1$, and its exact value is unknown. In an unrefereed solution posted on MathDB, Patel proved $K_2>\PatelLB$. His argument combines Knese's bound in the higher degrees with three inequalities for the coefficients of degrees two and three, one of which comes from the unitary realization of Schur functions on the bidisc. We present his argument in full detail, confirm that it is correct, and check its exact computation. Knese's bound in degree $n$ carries the factor $\sqrt n$, and this factor can be replaced by the Sidon constant of $\{0,1,\dots,n\}$, which is smaller than $\sqrt n$ for every $n\ge5$ by a theorem of Shapiro. We prove the computer-assisted bounds $S(\{0,1,\dots,5\})\le\FiveOwn$, $S(\{0,1,\dots,6\})\le\SixOwn$ and $S(\{0,1,\dots,7\})\le\SevenOwn$; the first is consistent with the exact value found by Ye et al. With these bounds we obtain $K_2>\Kbound$. The numerical steps use exact rational arithmetic and ball arithmetic. In the Lean proof assistant we also prove $K_2\ge\Kbound$, assuming the inequality from the unitary realization; formal proofs of the three Sidon bounds, and of $K_2\ge\LeanBound$ under the same assumption, rely on Lean's kernel instead of code generated by Lean's compiler.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For $d\ge1$ let $\D^d=\{z\in\C^d:|z_1|,\dots,|z_d|<1\}$ be the unit polydisc and let the Schur class $\mathcal S(\D^d)$ be the set of holomorphic maps $f\colon\D^d\to\overline{\D}$. For $f(z)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha z^\alpha$ and $r>0$ write $\|f_r\|_1=\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha|r^{|\alpha|}$, where $|\alpha|=\alpha_1+\dots+\alpha_d$. The Bohr radius of $\D^d$ is
\[
K_d=\sup\{r>0:\|f_r\|_1\le1\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal S(\D^d)\}.
\]
Bohr \cite{Boh14} proved that $K_1\ge1/6$. The sharp value $K_1=1/3$ was found independently by Riesz, Schur and Wiener, and Bohr's paper includes Wiener's proof (see \cite{BK97}). Boas and Khavinson \cite{BK97} introduced $K_d$ for $d\ge2$, proved $1/(3\sqrt d)<K_d<2\sqrt{(\log d)/d}$ and asked for the exact value of $K_d$ when $d>1$. Defant, Frerick, Ortega-Cerd\`a, Ouna\"ies and Seip \cite{DFOOS11} showed that $K_d$ has order $\sqrt{(\log d)/d}$, and Bayart, Pellegrino and Seoane-Sep\'ulveda \cite{BPS14} proved $K_d\sim\sqrt{(\log d)/d}$ as $d\to\infty$. No exact value of $K_d$ is known for any $d\ge2$.

For the bidisc, functions of one variable show $K_2\le1/3$. Knese \cite[Corollary~1.2]{Kne25} proved $K_2\ge\KneseVal$: by his Theorem~1.1 and And\^o's inequality, $K_2$ is at least the root $\KneseRoot$ of $\sum_{k\ge1}\sqrt k\,r^k=1/2$ (his corrigendum \cite{Kne26} corrects Theorem~1.4 of \cite{Kne25}). Baran, Pikul, Woerdeman and Wojtylak \cite[Theorem~6.4]{BPWW26} proved $K_2<\BPWWUB$, and Griego \cite{Gri26} lowered this to $K_2<\GriegoUB$.

Patel \cite{PatMDB} posted two bounds in an unrefereed solution to a problem on MathDB. For the upper bound he exhibits a rational inner function, and the post describes an exact integer computation that gives $K_2<\PatelUB$ and states its outcome. His reproducibility package \cite{PatZen} contains two programs that carry it out and a Lean proof. For the lower bound, a rotation and the substitution $H=(1-f)/(1-f(0))$ reduce the problem to the coefficients of holomorphic functions $H$ on $\D^2$ with $\operatorname{Re}H>0$ and $H(0)=1$. Patel proves three inequalities for the coefficients of $H$ of degrees two and three. The first two follow from the one-variable Carath\'eodory inequality on slices, the second with the help of a positive semidefinite Toeplitz form. The third couples the two degrees and comes from the unitary realization of Schur functions on the bidisc \cite{Agl90,BT98}, which rests on And\^o's inequality \cite{And63}. Using Knese's estimate \cite[Lemma~2.1]{Kne25} in degrees $n\ge4$, he then obtains $K_2>\PatelLB$ by an exact rational computation.

A second route uses Sidon constants. For a finite set $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z$ the Sidon constant $S(\Lambda)$ is the least $C$ with
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|a_\lambda|\le C\max_{|w|=1}\Bigl|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_\lambda w^\lambda\Bigr|\qquad(a_\lambda\in\C).
\]
Bernal-Gonz\'alez, Cabana, Garc\'ia, Maestre, Mu\~noz-Fern\'andez and Seoane-Sep\'ulveda \cite[Theorem~3.3]{BCGMMS21} showed that $K_d$ is at least the root of $\sum_{k\ge1}S(k,d)\,r^k=1/2$, where $S(k,d)$ is the Sidon constant of the $k$-homogeneous polynomials in $d$ variables. We write
\[
S_k=S(\{0,1,\dots,k\})\qquad(k\ge1).
\]
For $d=2$, $S(k,2)=S_k$ (Remark~\ref{rem:skd}). Newman's bound $S_k\le\sqrt k$ \cite[Chapter~3]{Sha51} (see also \cite[(1)]{Neu26}) turns this equation into Knese's, and Shapiro \cite{Sha51} showed that $S_k=\sqrt k$ exactly when $k\in\{1,2,4\}$. Neuwirth conjectured $S_3=5/3$ \cite{Neu01} and later proved $S_3\ge5/3$ \cite{Neu26}. Yu et al.\ proved $S_3\le5/3$, and hence $S_3=5/3$, with a formalized proof \cite{Yu26}, and Ye et al.\ determined $S_5=\YeVal$ in a computer-assisted preprint \cite{Ye26}. With these values and $S_k\le\sqrt k$ for $k\ge6$, the equation of \cite{BCGMMS21} gives $K_2\ge\SidonRoot$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:sidon}). If the value of Ye et al.\ and Newman's bounds for $S_6$ and $S_7$ are replaced by the bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:s5} for $S_5$, $S_6$ and $S_7$, the equation gives $K_2\ge0.301370$. Patel's coupling of degrees two and three goes beyond this.

In this paper we present Patel's argument with full details and improve its result. His post contains all the steps, and we have also checked his exact computation. Our improvement starts from the following observation. The slice argument behind Patel's inequalities also shows that the coefficients of degree $n$ of $H$ have absolute sum at most $2S_n$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:slice}), which is the Carath\'eodory form of \cite[Lemma~3.1]{BCGMMS21}. Patel uses Knese's estimate $2\sqrt n$ in the degrees $n\ge4$, and there $2S_n$ is smaller wherever $S_n<\sqrt n$. Since $S_4=2=\sqrt4$, the first such degree is $n=5$, and by Shapiro's theorem every larger degree qualifies. We prove $S_5\le\FiveOwn$, $S_6\le\SixOwn$ and $S_7\le\SevenOwn$ with computer-assisted certificates (Proposition~\ref{prop:s5}). The exact value of $S_5$ found by Ye et al.\ \cite{Ye26} is consistent with the first bound and would serve equally well, but we use our own bound to keep the proof independent of that computation. With these bounds, an exact computation gives the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
$K_2>\Kbound$.
\end{theorem}

The gain over Patel's bound, about $8\cdot10^{-5}$, comes from the terms of degrees five, six and seven. Together with Patel's upper bound, which is likewise unrefereed, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives $\Kbound<K_2<\PatelUB$.

In Section~\ref{sec:setup} we set up the normalization and prove the slice estimate, including Carath\'eodory's inequality on slices. In Section~\ref{sec:patel} we write out Patel's treatment of the coefficients of degrees two and three, including the Gram argument behind the cubic inequality, the unitary extension and the identities of the realization. Section~\ref{sec:values} treats the Sidon constants and proves the bounds for $S_5$, $S_6$ and $S_7$, Section~\ref{sec:proof} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and Section~\ref{sec:verif} lists the computer checks. Section~\ref{sec:lean} describes the formal proofs in Lean~4. Throughout, lower bounds are rounded down and upper bounds up.

\section{The Carath\'eodory normalization and slices}\label{sec:setup}

Let $\Pclass$ be the set of holomorphic functions $H$ on $\D^2$ with $\operatorname{Re}H>0$ and $H(0)=1$. We write
\[
H(z)=1+\sum_{n\ge1}H_n(z),\qquad H_n(z)=\sum_{j+k=n}h_{jk}z_1^jz_2^k,
\]
and set $\|H_n\|_1=\sum_{j+k=n}|h_{jk}|$ and $M_r(H)=\sum_{n\ge1}\|H_n\|_1\,r^n$.

Patel \cite{PatMDB} uses the following reduction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:norm}
Let $0<r<1$. If $M_r(H)\le1$ for every $H\in\Pclass$, then $K_2\ge r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Constant functions satisfy $\|f_r\|_1\le1$. Let $f\in\mathcal S(\D^2)$ be nonconstant. By the maximum principle $|f|<1$ on $\D^2$. Multiplying $f$ by a unimodular constant does not change $\|f_r\|_1$, so we may assume that $t=f(0)$ lies in $[0,1)$. Then $H=(1-f)/(1-t)$ lies in $\Pclass$. Its coefficients of positive degree are those of $f$ multiplied by $-1/(1-t)$. Hence $\|f_r\|_1=t+(1-t)M_r(H)\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:converse}
The normalization loses nothing: if $r<K_2$, then $M_r(H)\le1$ for every $H\in\Pclass$. Indeed, for $0<s<1$ the function $z\mapsto H(sz)$ maps $\overline{\D}^2$ into a compact subset of the right half-plane, hence into a disc $\{w:|w-\rho|<\rho\}$ with $\rho\ge1$. Then $f(z)=1-H(sz)/\rho$ lies in $\mathcal S(\D^2)$ with $f(0)=1-1/\rho$, and $\|f_r\|_1\le1$ becomes $\sum_n\|H_n\|_1\,s^nr^n\le1$. Let $s\to1$. Thus $K_2=\sup\{r:M_r(H)\le1\text{ for all }H\in\Pclass\}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:slice}
Let $H\in\Pclass$ and $n\ge1$. Then $|H_n(\zeta)|\le2$ for $\zeta\in\T^2$, and $\|H_n\|_1\le2S_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\zeta\in\T^2$. The function $\varphi(\lambda)=H(\lambda\zeta)=1+\sum_{m\ge1}H_m(\zeta)\lambda^m$ is holomorphic on $\D$ with $\operatorname{Re}\varphi>0$. For $0<\rho<1$,
\[
H_n(\zeta)\rho^n=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{Re}\varphi(\rho e^{i\theta})\,e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta,
\]
so $|H_n(\zeta)|\rho^n\le2\operatorname{Re}\varphi(0)=2$. Letting $\rho\to1$, we obtain Carath\'eodory's inequality $|H_n(\zeta)|\le2$. Now write $H_n(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)=\zeta_2^n\,q_n(\zeta_1/\zeta_2)$ with $q_n(w)=\sum_{j=0}^nh_{j,n-j}w^j$. As $\zeta$ runs over $\T^2$, the quotient $\zeta_1/\zeta_2$ runs over $\T$ and $|H_n(\zeta)|=|q_n(\zeta_1/\zeta_2)|$. Hence $\max_\T|q_n|\le2$, and by the definition of $S_n$,
\[
\|H_n\|_1=\sum_{j=0}^n|h_{j,n-j}|\le S_n\max_\T|q_n|\le2S_n.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:skd}
The same identification shows that $S(k,2)=S_k$: on $\T^2$ a $k$-homogeneous polynomial in two variables is, up to the factor $\zeta_2^k$, a polynomial in $\zeta_1/\zeta_2$ of degree at most $k$. With this identification, the next corollary is the case $d=2$ of \cite[Theorem~3.3]{BCGMMS21}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:sidon}
If $0<r<1$ and $\sum_{k\ge1}S_kr^k\le1/2$, then $K_2\ge r$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:slice}, $M_r(H)\le2\sum_{k\ge1}S_kr^k\le1$ for every $H\in\Pclass$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:norm}.
\end{proof}

In \cite{BCGMMS21} the corresponding estimate is proved for $f$ itself. There Wiener's inequality takes the place of Carath\'eodory's: the Taylor coefficients $\hat g(k)$ of a holomorphic $g\colon\D\to\overline{\D}$ satisfy $|\hat g(k)|\le1-|g(0)|^2$ for $k\ge1$. Patel derives the bound $\|H_n\|_1\le2\sqrt n$ for all $H\in\Pclass$ from Knese's estimate \cite[Lemma~2.1]{Kne25}, by a limiting argument with $(1-\varepsilon H)/(1+\varepsilon H)$ \cite{PatMDB}. Lemma~\ref{lem:slice} with Newman's bound gives it directly.

\section{The coefficients of degrees two and three}\label{sec:patel}

Throughout this section $H\in\Pclass$, and
\[
A_2=|h_{20}|+|h_{02}|,\quad B_2=|h_{11}|,\qquad A_3=|h_{30}|+|h_{03}|,\quad B_3=|h_{21}|+|h_{12}|,
\]
so that $\|H_2\|_1=A_2+B_2$ and $\|H_3\|_1=A_3+B_3$. The three inequalities and the way they are combined are due to Patel \cite{PatMDB}. Inner products are linear in the first argument, $\langle x,y\rangle=y^*x$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quad}
$A_2^2+B_2^2\le4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $\zeta_0\in\T$ such that $h_{20}\zeta_0^2$ and $h_{02}$ have the same argument, and put $a=h_{20}\zeta_0^2+h_{02}$ and $b=h_{11}\zeta_0$. Then $|a|=A_2$, $|b|=B_2$ and $H_2(\pm\zeta_0,1)=a\pm b$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:slice}, $|a\pm b|\le2$, and therefore $|a|^2+|b|^2=\tfrac12(|a+b|^2+|a-b|^2)\le4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:toeplitz}
Let $R$ be a positive semidefinite Hermitian Toeplitz matrix of size $k+1$, and let $q(\zeta)=\sum_{j=0}^kc_j\zeta^j$. Then $c^*Rc\le R_{00}\max_\T|q|^2$, where $c=(c_0,\dots,c_k)^{\mathsf T}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_0,\dots,e_k$ be vectors in $\C^{k+1}$ with $\langle e_l,e_j\rangle=R_{jl}$, for example the columns of $R^{1/2}$. Since $R$ is Toeplitz, the tuples $(e_0,\dots,e_{k-1})$ and $(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ have the same Gram matrix. Hence $e_j\mapsto e_{j+1}$ $(0\le j<k)$ extends to a well-defined linear isometry from the span of $e_0,\dots,e_{k-1}$ onto the span of $e_1,\dots,e_k$, and then to a unitary operator $\Omega$ on the span of all the $e_j$. Then $e_j=\Omega^je_0$, so $\sum_jc_je_j=q(\Omega)e_0$. As $\Omega$ is unitary, $\|q(\Omega)\|\le\max_\T|q|$, and
\[
c^*Rc=\Bigl\|\sum_jc_je_j\Bigr\|^2=\|q(\Omega)e_0\|^2\le\max_\T|q|^2\,\|e_0\|^2=R_{00}\max_\T|q|^2.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:toeplitz} is a consequence of the Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz theorem. Patel's post contains this argument for $k=3$, and Ye et al.\ prove it in the same way \cite[Lemma~3.1]{Ye26}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cubic}
Let $p(\zeta)=\sum_{j=0}^3c_j\zeta^j$ satisfy $\max_\T|p|\le1$. Then
\[
35\bigl(|c_0|+|c_3|\bigr)^2+28\bigl(|c_1|+|c_2|\bigr)^2\le44.
\]
Consequently $35A_3^2+28B_3^2\le176$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Replacing $p(\zeta)$ by $p(e^{i\theta}\zeta)$ for a suitable $\theta$, we may assume that $c_1$ and $c_2$ have the same argument. Choose $\xi\in\T$ with $|\bar\xi c_0+c_3|=|c_0|+|c_3|$, and let
\[
u=(3,4,-4,-3)^{\mathsf T},\qquad v=(0,1,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad w=(\xi,0,0,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad R=uu^*+28vv^*+35ww^*.
\]
The matrix $R$ is positive semidefinite. Since $|\xi|=1$, it is also Toeplitz, with first row $(44,12,-12,-9+35\xi)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:toeplitz}, with $c=(c_0,\dots,c_3)^{\mathsf T}$,
\[
|u^*c|^2+28|v^*c|^2+35|w^*c|^2=c^*Rc\le44.
\]
Here $|v^*c|=|c_1+c_2|=|c_1|+|c_2|$ and $|w^*c|=|\bar\xi c_0+c_3|=|c_0|+|c_3|$, which proves the first claim. For the second, apply it to $p=q_3/2$ from the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:slice}. Then $|c_0|+|c_3|=A_3/2$ and $|c_1|+|c_2|=B_3/2$.
\end{proof}

Patel describes this argument as a weighted variant of the identity in the proof of $S_3=5/3$ by Yu et al.\ \cite{Yu26}. The proof above does not depend on \cite{Yu26}, and it extends to a one-parameter family. For $-1\le t\le2$ and $u_t=(t,1,-1,-t)^{\mathsf T}$, the matrix $u_tu_t^*+(t+1)vv^*+(2+t-t^2)ww^*$ is positive semidefinite and Toeplitz, with first row $(2+t,t,-t,-t^2+(2+t-t^2)\xi)$, so the same argument shows
\[
(2+t-t^2)\bigl(|c_0|+|c_3|\bigr)^2+(t+1)\bigl(|c_1|+|c_2|\bigr)^2\le2+t.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic} is the case $t=3/4$. The case $t=1/2$ is the inequality $9(|c_0|+|c_3|)^2+6(|c_1|+|c_2|)^2\le10$, from which Yu et al.\ obtain $S_3\le5/3$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality \cite[(6)]{Yu26}. On its own, Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic} gives only $\|H_3\|_1\le\sqrt{396/35}\approx3.364$, more than $2S_3=10/3$. It is used together with the next inequality, which bounds $A_3$ in terms of $B_2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:couple}
$A_3+\tfrac12B_2^2\le2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the transfer-function realization of Schur functions on the bidisc. The Schur--Agler class consists of the $g\in\mathcal S(\D^2)$ with $\|g(X_1,X_2)\|\le1$ for every pair of commuting strict contractions $X_1$, $X_2$ on a Hilbert space. By And\^o's inequality \cite{And63}, every $g\in\mathcal S(\D^2)$ belongs to this class. By Agler's theorem \cite{Agl90} (see also \cite{BT98,AM02}) there are a Hilbert space $\mathcal K$, complementary orthogonal projections $P$ and $Q$ on $\mathcal K$, and an isometry $V=\begin{psmallmatrix}\kappa&x^*\\y&W\end{psmallmatrix}$ of $\C\oplus\mathcal K$, the colligation, such that
\[
g(z)=\kappa+x^*Z\bigl(I-WZ\bigr)^{-1}y,\qquad Z=z_1P+z_2Q\qquad(z\in\D^2).
\]
For $z\in\D^2$ we have $\|Z\|\le\max_j|z_j|<1$, so $I-WZ$ and the other operators $I-ZW$, $I-ZU$ inverted below are invertible.
We may take $V$ unitary, which matters if the realization is given with an isometric colligation, as in \cite[Section~2]{Kne25}. Indeed, the operator $\begin{psmallmatrix}V&I-VV^*\\0&V^*\end{psmallmatrix}$ on $(\C\oplus\mathcal K)\oplus(\C\oplus\mathcal K)$ is unitary and agrees with $V$ on the first summand. We use it as the new colligation, with state space $\mathcal K\oplus(\C\oplus\mathcal K)$ and projections $P\oplus I$ and $Q\oplus0$. The new $y$ is the old one, the new $W$ and $x^*$ agree with the old ones on $\mathcal K$, and the new projections map $\mathcal K$ into itself, so each term $x^*Z(WZ)^my$ of the expansion of $g$ is unchanged. From now on $\mathcal K$, $V$, $x$, $y$, $W$, $P$ and $Q$ denote the new data.

Apply this to $g=(H-1)/(H+1)$, which lies in $\mathcal S(\D^2)$ and vanishes at $0$. Then $\kappa=0$, and since $V$ is unitary,
\[
\|x\|=\|y\|=1,\qquad Wx=0,\qquad W^*y=0,\qquad xx^*+W^*W=I,\qquad yy^*+WW^*=I.
\]
Put $U=W+yx^*$. These identities imply $U^*U=UU^*=I$. Let $G=I-ZW$. Then $Gx=x$ and $x^*G^{-1}Zy=x^*Z(I-WZ)^{-1}y=g$. Since $I-ZU=G-Zyx^*$ is a rank-one perturbation of $G$ and $1-g\ne0$,
\[
x^*(I-ZU)^{-1}x=x^*G^{-1}x+\frac{(x^*G^{-1}Zy)(x^*G^{-1}x)}{1-x^*G^{-1}Zy}=1+\frac{g}{1-g}=\frac1{1-g}.
\]
Hence $x^*(I+ZU)(I-ZU)^{-1}x=2x^*(I-ZU)^{-1}x-1=(1+g)/(1-g)=H$, and expanding the resolvent,
\[
H_n(z)=2x^*(ZU)^nx\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Relative to $\mathcal K=P\mathcal K\oplus Q\mathcal K$ write
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}U_{11}&U_{12}\\U_{21}&U_{22}\end{pmatrix},\qquad x=\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix},\qquad Ux=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde a\\\tilde b\end{pmatrix},
\]
so that $\|a\|^2+\|b\|^2=\|\tilde a\|^2+\|\tilde b\|^2=1$. Collecting the monomials $z_1z_2$, $z_1^3$ and $z_2^3$ in $2x^*(ZU)^nx$, we find
\[
\tfrac12h_{11}=a^*U_{12}\tilde b+b^*U_{21}\tilde a,\qquad\tfrac12h_{30}=a^*U_{11}^2\tilde a,\qquad\tfrac12h_{03}=b^*U_{22}^2\tilde b.
\]
Put $\sigma=\|U_{11}^*a\|^2+\|U_{22}^*b\|^2$ and $\tau=\|U_{11}\tilde a\|^2+\|U_{22}\tilde b\|^2$. Since $U^*$ is an isometry, $\|U_{11}^*a\|^2+\|U_{12}^*a\|^2=\|a\|^2$ and $\|U_{21}^*b\|^2+\|U_{22}^*b\|^2=\|b\|^2$, so $\|U_{12}^*a\|^2+\|U_{21}^*b\|^2=1-\sigma$. Since $U$ is an isometry, $\|U_{12}\tilde b\|^2+\|U_{21}\tilde a\|^2=1-\tau$ in the same way. Applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to $\tfrac12h_{11}=\langle\tilde b,U_{12}^*a\rangle+\langle\tilde a,U_{21}^*b\rangle=\langle U_{12}\tilde b,a\rangle+\langle U_{21}\tilde a,b\rangle$ in the two groupings, we obtain
\[
\tfrac14B_2^2\le1-\sigma\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tfrac14B_2^2\le1-\tau.
\]
Finally $\tfrac12|h_{30}|=|\langle U_{11}\tilde a,U_{11}^*a\rangle|\le\tfrac12(\|U_{11}^*a\|^2+\|U_{11}\tilde a\|^2)$, and likewise for $h_{03}$, so $A_3\le\sigma+\tau\le2-\tfrac12B_2^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:quad} and~\ref{lem:couple} are sharp. Equality holds in both for $H=(1+z_1)/(1-z_1)$, where $A_2=A_3=2$ and $B_2=0$, and for $H=(1+z_1z_2)/(1-z_1z_2)$, where $A_2=A_3=0$ and $B_2=2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:combine}
Let $\alpha,\beta>0$, $\gamma\ge0$ and $r>0$, and put
\[
\Bfun(r)=4\alpha+176\beta+2\gamma+\frac1{4\alpha}+\frac1{4\alpha+2\gamma}+\frac{(r-\gamma)^2}{140\beta}+\frac{r^2}{112\beta}.
\]
Then $\|H_2\|_1+r\|H_3\|_1\le\Bfun(r)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the squares verifies the identity
\begin{align*}
\Bfun(r)-\bigl(A_2+B_2+r(A_3+B_3)\bigr)={}&\alpha\bigl(4-A_2^2-B_2^2\bigr)+\beta\bigl(176-35A_3^2-28B_3^2\bigr)+\gamma\bigl(2-A_3-\tfrac12B_2^2\bigr)\\
&+\alpha\Bigl(A_2-\frac1{2\alpha}\Bigr)^2+\Bigl(\alpha+\frac\gamma2\Bigr)\Bigl(B_2-\frac1{2\alpha+\gamma}\Bigr)^2\\
&+35\beta\Bigl(A_3-\frac{r-\gamma}{70\beta}\Bigr)^2+28\beta\Bigl(B_3-\frac r{56\beta}\Bigr)^2.
\end{align*}
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:quad}, \ref{lem:cubic} and~\ref{lem:couple}, each term on the right is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

Thus $\Bfun(r)$ is the Lagrangian bound, with multipliers $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$, for the maximum of $A_2+B_2+r(A_3+B_3)$ under the three inequalities of Lemmas~\ref{lem:quad}, \ref{lem:cubic} and~\ref{lem:couple}.

Its proof rests on the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dual}
Let $k\ge1$. For $w\in\C^{k+1}$ put
\[
\nu(w)=\max\Bigl\{\operatorname{Re}\sum_{j=0}^k\bar w_jc_j:\ \max_\T\Bigl|\sum_{j=0}^kc_jz^j\Bigr|\le1\Bigr\}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $S_k=\max\{\nu(w):w_0=w_1=1,\ |w_j|=1\ (2\le j\le k)\}$.
\item $\nu$ is convex.
\item Let $y\in\C^k$, and let $T(y)$ be the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix with diagonal entries $1$ and entries $T(y)_{jl}=y_{l-j}$ for $l>j$. If $T(y)$ is positive definite, then $\nu(w)^2\le w^*T(y)^{-1}w$.
\item For $0<\delta\le\pi/4$, the arc $\{e^{i\varphi}:|\varphi-\varphi_0|\le\delta\}$ lies in the triangle with vertices $e^{i(\varphi_0-\delta)}$, $e^{i(\varphi_0+\delta)}$ and $e^{i\varphi_0}/\cos\delta$.
\item $\nu(\bar w)=\nu(w)$, where $\bar w=(\bar w_0,\dots,\bar w_k)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Write $q(z)=\sum_jc_jz^j$. If $|w_j|=1$ for all $j$, then $\nu(w)\le\max\{\sum_j|c_j|:\max_\T|q|\le1\}=S_k$. Equality holds when $c$ attains this maximum and $w_j=c_j/|c_j|$ (any unimodular number if $c_j=0$). The substitution $c_j\mapsto c_je^{i(\psi+j\theta)}$ replaces $q(z)$ by $e^{i\psi}q(e^{i\theta}z)$ and $w_j$ by $w_je^{-i(\psi+j\theta)}$ without changing $\nu$, and a choice of $\psi$ and $\theta$ makes $w_0=w_1=1$.
(b) $\nu$ is a supremum of real-linear functions of $w$.
(c) By Lemma~\ref{lem:toeplitz}, $c^*T(y)c\le\max_\T|q|^2$ for $c=(c_0,\dots,c_k)^{\mathsf T}$. Hence, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
\operatorname{Re}w^*c\le\bigl(w^*T(y)^{-1}w\bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(c^*T(y)c\bigr)^{1/2}\le\bigl(w^*T(y)^{-1}w\bigr)^{1/2}\max_\T|q|.
\]
(d) After a rotation, $\varphi_0=0$. The triangle is $\{u\in\C:\operatorname{Re}u\ge\cos\delta,\ \operatorname{Re}(ue^{-i\delta})\le1,\ \operatorname{Re}(ue^{i\delta})\le1\}$, and $u=e^{i\varphi}$ with $|\varphi|\le\delta$ satisfies $\cos\varphi\ge\cos\delta$ and $\cos(\varphi\mp\delta)\le1$.
(e) The map $c\mapsto\bar c$ maps the set of admissible $c$ onto itself, since $\sum_j\bar c_jz^j=\overline{q(\bar z)}$, and $\operatorname{Re}\sum_jw_j\bar c_j=\operatorname{Re}\sum_j\bar w_jc_j$. Hence $\nu(\bar w)=\max\operatorname{Re}\sum_jw_jc_j=\max\operatorname{Re}\sum_jw_j\bar c_j=\nu(w)$.
\end{proof}

Parts (a) and (c) are variants of the reduction to phase functionals and of the Toeplitz domination bound used by Ye et al.\ \cite[(14) and Proposition~3.2]{Ye26}, who normalize the phases differently \cite[(17)]{Ye26}. Proposition~\ref{prop:s5} also differs from their proof in the covering of the phase space.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:s5}
$S_5\le\FiveOwn$, $S_6\le\SixOwn$ and $S_7\le\SevenOwn$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof \textup{(computer-assisted)}]
Let $(k,b_k)$ be $(5,\FiveOwn)$, $(6,\SixOwn)$ or $(7,\SevenOwn)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(a) it suffices to show that $\nu(1,1,e^{i\varphi_2},\dots,e^{i\varphi_k})\le b_k$ for $(\varphi_2,\dots,\varphi_k)\in[-\pi,\pi]^{k-1}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(e), which replaces $(\varphi_2,\dots,\varphi_k)$ by $(-\varphi_2,\dots,-\varphi_k)$, we may also assume $\varphi_2\ge0$. For $k=5$ and $k=6$ the certificate covers $[-\pi,\pi]^{k-1}$ by boxes, obtained from a uniform grid of $8^{k-1}$ boxes with half-widths $\pi/8$ by repeated bisection. For $k=7$ it covers $[0,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]^5$ in the same way, starting from a uniform grid of $2\cdot4^5$ boxes with half-widths $\pi/4$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(d), the vectors $(1,1,e^{i\varphi_2},\dots,e^{i\varphi_k})$ with $\varphi$ in the box lie in a product of $k-1$ triangles. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(b), the maximum of $\nu$ over this product is attained at one of its $3^{k-1}$ vertices. For each vertex $v$ that occurs, the certificate gives $y\in\C^k$ with $T(y)$ positive definite and $v^*T(y)^{-1}v<b_k^2$, so that $\nu(v)<b_k$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(c). A vertex with a coordinate $-1$ occurs with two labels, phase $-\pi$ and phase $\pi$, and has a certificate for each. Table~\ref{tab:certs} gives the sizes of the three certificates, and Section~\ref{sec:verif} describes the check.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

Let $N=\TailN$. Let $s_4=2$, $s_5=\FiveOwn$, $s_6=\SixOwn$, $s_7=\SevenOwn$, and $s_n=\lceil10^8\sqrt n\,\rceil/10^8$ for $8\le n\le N$. By Table~\ref{tab:sidon} and Proposition~\ref{prop:s5}, $S_n\le s_n$ for $4\le n\le N$, and $S_n\le\sqrt n\le n$ for all $n$. Let $H\in\Pclass$, let $0<r<1$, and fix $\alpha,\beta>0$ and $\gamma\ge0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:slice} bounds $\|H_n\|_1$ by $2$ for $n=1$, by $2s_n$ for $4\le n\le N$ and by $2n$ for $n>N$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:combine} bounds $\|H_2\|_1+r\|H_3\|_1$. Summing the series $\sum_{n>N}nr^n=r^{N+1}(N+1-Nr)/(1-r)^2$, we obtain a majorant of the form that Patel uses \cite{PatMDB}:
\[
M_r(H)\le E(r):=2r+r^2\Bfun(r)+2\sum_{n=4}^{N}s_nr^n+\frac{2r^{N+1}(N+1-Nr)}{(1-r)^2}.
\]
We take
\[
r_*=\rstar,\qquad\alpha=\ParA,\qquad\beta=\ParB,\qquad\gamma=\ParG.
\]
All quantities in $E(r_*)$ are then rational, and an exact computation shows $E(r_*)<\Evalue<1$. Since $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are fixed, $E$ is continuous on $(0,1)$, so there is $r'>r_*$ with $E(r')<1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:norm}, as in Patel's argument, $K_2\ge r'>r_*$, which proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

With Patel's parameters $\alpha=264631/800000$, $\beta=249627/10^8$ and $\gamma=10197021/10^8$, with $N=20$ and with $s_n=\lceil10^8\sqrt n\,\rceil/10^8$ for all $4\le n\le20$, the function $E$ is the rational majorant that Patel evaluates in \cite{PatMDB}. Its value at his radius $r_P=15079379/(5\cdot10^7)$ is less than $\EvaluePatel$, below his bound $1-1/(5\cdot10^7)$ (Table~\ref{tab:checks}).

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verif}

Four programs check the computations, and a fifth checks Patel's computation. All of them are in the archive listed under Data availability, together with the certificates, the programs that produced them and the output of all runs.

The first program checks the certificate for $S_5$ in ball arithmetic, using Arb \cite{Joh17} through python-flint \cite{pyflint} at a precision of $128$ bits. The certificate stores the entries of each $y$ as decimal numerals, which Python parses into the nearest binary64 numbers. These binary64 numbers are the certificate, and the program reads them exactly into balls. It rebuilds the boxes from the uniform grid and the recorded bisections, so the boxes cover $[-\pi,\pi]^4$ by construction. For each box it forms the $81$ vertices of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(d) from rational multiples of $\pi$. For each vertex label it checks that the leading principal minors of $T(y)$ are positive and that $v^*T(y)^{-1}v<\FiveOwn^2$, solving the linear system in ball arithmetic. The run takes \CheckTime{} and $0.6$ GB of memory on one core of an x86-64 server.

The certificate for $S_6$ occupies about $3.4$ GB and is split into $48$ files, and the certificate for $S_7$ occupies about \SizeSeven{} GB and is split into $\NFilesSeven$ files. Each file contains some cells of the initial grid, their subtrees and the vertex certificates they need. The second program carries out the checks of the first program on one such file and lists the cells it has covered, together with the name of the file and its SHA-256 checksum. The third reads these lists and checks that the $48$ files together cover each of the $8^5$ cells exactly once, and likewise for the $\NFilesSeven$ files and the $2\cdot4^5$ cells of the certificate for $S_7$. It recomputes the checksum of each file named in the lists and compares it with the listed one, but it trusts that the lists come from runs of the second program. The logs in the archive record such a run. Each of the $48$ runs of the second program takes under a minute and about $0.7$ GB of memory on one core, and each of the $\NFilesSeven$ runs for $S_7$ takes \TimeSeven.

The fourth program evaluates $E(r_*)$ of Section~\ref{sec:proof} in exact rational arithmetic and compares it with $1$. The fifth checks, in exact rational arithmetic, that $\Bfun(r_P)$ equals the fraction displayed in \cite{PatMDB}, that his table of rational upper bounds for $\sqrt n$ consists of the integers $\lceil10^8\sqrt n\,\rceil$, that the two parts of his majorant lie below the two rational bounds he displays, and that the sum of these bounds is below $1-1/(5\cdot10^7)$. \section{Formal verification}\label{sec:lean}

We also prove the bound $K_2\ge\Kbound$ in the Lean~4 proof assistant \cite{Lean4}, with the Mathlib library \cite{Mathlib}. The formal proof has one hypothesis, the inequality of Lemma~\ref{lem:couple}. Its proof rests on And\^o's inequality and Agler's realization, which are not in Mathlib. The formal statements use Mathlib's definitions. A family $c\colon\mathbb N^2\to\C$ is admissible if the series $\sum_\alpha c_\alpha z^\alpha$ converges absolutely at every point of $\D^2$ and its value there has modulus less than $1$. Bohr's inequality holds at $r$ if $\sum_{\alpha\in F}|c_\alpha|r^{|\alpha|}\le1$ for every admissible family $c$ and every finite set $F\subset\mathbb N^2$, and $K_2$ is the supremum of the radii $r>0$ at which it holds. It agrees with the definition in the introduction: a nonconstant $f\in\mathcal S(\D^2)$ maps $\D^2$ into $\D$, and a constant of modulus $1$ satisfies Bohr's inequality with equality. The hypothesis states $A_3+\tfrac12B_2^2\le2$ for every family $h$ with $h_{00}=1$ whose series converges absolutely on $\D^2$ to values with positive real part, that is, for the coefficients of every $H\in\Pclass$. The theorem \lean{Bohr59aN7.K2_ge_N7} states that this hypothesis implies $K_2\ge\Kbound$.

The formal proof follows Sections~\ref{sec:setup} to~\ref{sec:proof}. It proves Carath\'eodory's inequality on slices, Lemmas~\ref{lem:slice}, \ref{lem:quad}, \ref{lem:toeplitz}, \ref{lem:cubic} and~\ref{lem:combine}, Newman's bound $S_n\le\sqrt n$, the reduction of Section~\ref{sec:proof} and the exact evaluation of the majorant. The bounds $S_5\le\FiveOwn$, $S_6\le\SixOwn$ and $S_7\le\SevenOwn$ come from the certificates of Proposition~\ref{prop:s5}. A checking program written in Lean validates each part of a certificate in exact rational arithmetic and ball arithmetic, and a Lean proof shows that whenever it accepts a part, the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(c) holds on the boxes that the part covers. The certificate for $S_7$ covers only the phases with $\varphi_2\in[0,\pi]$, so for its parts the program checks the cells of that half of the domain, and the formal proof covers the other half with Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(e). Our scripts convert the certificates into Lean source files. None of these files needs to be trusted, since the correctness proof holds for every input that the program accepts.

The runs of the checking program on the $\NLEANCHECKS$ parts of the certificates, one for $S_5$, $48$ for $S_6$ and $\NFilesSeven$ for $S_7$, are evaluated by \lean{native_decide}, which executes compiled code. The trusted base therefore includes the Lean compiler, the C compiler that it uses, the Lean runtime and the compiled core-library functions that the checks call. Besides the standard axioms \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound}, the theorem \lean{K2_ge_N7} depends on $\NLEANCHECKS$ auxiliary axioms, one for each part, each stating that the compiled check returns true. The other parts of the proof use only the standard axioms. Among them are the theorems \lean{Bohr59a.K2_ge_patel}, which proves Patel's bound $K_2\ge\PatelLB$ under the same hypothesis, and \lean{Bohr59a.K2_ge_of_sidon7}, which derives $K_2\ge\Kbound$ from the hypothesis and the three Sidon bounds. The checking program contains no definitions marked \lean{implemented_by}, \lean{extern}, \lean{unsafe}, \lean{opaque} or \lean{partial}. The Lean files, for Lean~4 version \texttt{\LEANVERSION} and Mathlib commit \texttt{\MATHLIBCOMMIT}, are in the directory \texttt{lean/} of the archive.

A second formal proof relies on Lean's kernel instead of code generated by Lean's compiler. Its checking program uses only integers and structural recursion, so that the Lean kernel can evaluate it, and it is again proved correct in Lean. For each vertex of the certificates for $S_5$ and $S_6$, one of its vertex certificates becomes a theorem checked by the kernel, and further theorems check the boxes of each cell. This proof does not use the certificate for $S_7$ and gives the weaker bound $K_2\ge\LeanBound$ under the same hypothesis (theorem \lean{Bohr59aK.K2_ge_K}). It uses only the standard axioms, and its trusted base is Lean's kernel, including the kernel's built-in arithmetic on natural numbers. Its Lean files are in the directory \texttt{lean\_kernel/} of the archive.

The same approach also proves the three bounds for Sidon constants with every check run in the kernel. The theorems \lean{Bohr59aK.sidon5K} and \lean{Bohr59aK.sidon6K} of the second proof state $S_5\le\FiveOwn$ and $S_6\le\SixOwn$, and the theorem \lean{Bohr59aK.sidon7K}, in a third Lean project in the directory \texttt{lean\_kernel\_s7/} of the archive, states $S_7\le\SevenOwn$. For $S_7$, one vertex certificate for each of the $\NVertKseven$ distinct vertex labels of the certificate for $S_7$ becomes a theorem checked by the kernel, and Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(e) halves the phase domain as in the first proof. The three theorems use the definitions of the first proof and only the standard axioms. Together with \lean{Bohr59a.K2_ge_of_sidon7} above, they show that, under this hypothesis, $K_2\ge\Kbound$ follows from four theorems in which every check is run by the kernel; these are not combined into a single Lean theorem. Building the $\NModulesKseven$ generated modules of the third project took about $\KsevenHours$ hours of single-threaded processes, almost all of them for the $46{,}634$ modules that hold the vertex theorems.\footnote{The third project was also checked again separately. All its generated modules were regenerated from the certificate byte for byte, a complete rebuild gave compiled files byte-identical to those of the first build, and none of the $94{,}556$ compiled modules declares an axiom. Lean's kernel replayed $50{,}254$ of the modules, namely all those without vertex theorems and every twentieth vertex module, and nanoda, an independent implementation of the kernel, accepted exports of $580$ sampled modules. The theorems that turn checked cells into the bound were accepted by Lean's kernel through leanchecker, by nanoda and by lean4lean, a second independent implementation; all three rejected copies in which proofs had been swapped. Altered vertex and cell data were rejected.}
